# House Nine Story and Setting

## Document purpose

This is the living story and setting document for the game currently built around
the `hub` level. It records the direction that is currently agreed upon, distinguishes
firm premises from provisional ideas, and provides constraints for writing dialogue,
building levels, and adding characters.

The exact title of the game, the name of the organization behind House Nine, the
present year, several individual societies, the ultimate escape route, and Elin's
uncle's final fate remain open.

## High concept

After the Second World War, a private international cabal of industrialists,
financiers, scientists, and wealthy families secretly constructed an underground
continuity facility called **House Nine**.

The founders expected atomic war or some other civilizational collapse. They did not
merely want to preserve humanity. They wanted complete control over the civilization
that would replace it. Adults were already shaped by cultures and institutions the
founders considered failures, so House Nine acquired orphaned, unwanted, trafficked,
or stolen infants and raised them inside isolated experimental societies.

No global catastrophe ever occurred. The ordinary world continued above House Nine,
while the project continued below it for generations.

In the present day, municipal technician James enters a disused subway service tunnel
to investigate an electrical irregularity. Reporter Elin enters the same tunnel using
secret instructions from her uncle, a House Nine maintenance worker who has decided to
expose or sabotage the project. Her uncle attempts to open a distant escape route.
Management responds by activating the facility's original total-lockdown system and
detonating charges that collapse the approach tunnels outside its blast doors.

James is caught in one collapse. Elin finds him injured, helps him recover, and conceals
the real reason she entered the tunnel. With the route behind them buried, they pass
through an outer staff annex and begin moving inward through the separate visions of
the future designed by House Nine's founders.

Their immediate objectives are simple:

1. Find Elin's uncle, or discover what happened to him.
2. Find a surviving route back to the surface.
3. Understand what House Nine is and what its collapse is doing to the people inside.

## Core identity

House Nine combines several kinds of horror:

- The physical danger and resource pressure of survival horror.
- Environmental investigation and item puzzles from point-and-click adventures.
- Branching conversations with people whose entire understanding of reality is at
  stake.
- A progression through competing artificial societies, each created as a wealthy
  patron's proposed cure for the failures of civilization.
- Paranoia based on indoctrination, incomplete information, concealed identities, and
  incompatible histories rather than literal shape-shifting.

The game is not about wandering through a dead laboratory and reading about everyone
who used to live there. James and Elin enter a living system at the moment its walls
begin to fail.

## Firm setting decisions

The following points should be treated as canon unless this document is deliberately
revised:

- House Nine was privately conceived, funded, built, and operated from the beginning.
- It was not a government shelter later repurposed for experiments.
- Its founders organized after the atomic bombings at the end of the Second World War.
- The facility has been expanded and technologically upgraded over many decades.
- Its visible technology may range from postwar machinery to 1980s, 1990s, and early
  2000s electronics.
- The present-day surface world has not been destroyed.
- James and Elin do not initially believe an apocalypse has occurred.
- House Nine's residents were acquired as infants or born inside the facility.
- Different resident populations are physically and socially isolated from one
  another.
- Outer personnel are also physically isolated from every resident society.
- Personnel do not enter occupied societies during normal operation and residents do
  not see personnel delivering supplies or performing maintenance.
- The existing `hub` map is principally an outer personnel and service annex, not one
  of the experimental societies.
- Elin is looking for her maintenance-worker uncle but initially lies about why she is
  in the subway tunnels.
- The uncle's actions elsewhere in House Nine cause management to activate the
  lockdown that collapses the tunnel around James.
- House Nine does not detect James and does not collapse the tunnel because of him.
- James and Elin will progress through multiple visions of an engineered future
  society before ultimately escaping or reaching a possible escape.

## Historical outline

### Formation

The atomic bombings of 1945 convince a group of wealthy international figures that
another world war will eventually destroy organized civilization. They regard existing
governments as both incapable of preventing that destruction and unworthy of shaping
what follows it.

The group forms a private continuity organization. Its public fronts may include
charities, orphanages, construction companies, medical foundations, and industrial
research concerns. These fronts allow it to acquire land, machinery, expertise,
children, and large quantities of supplies without revealing a single central project.

### Construction

House Nine is constructed beneath or alongside railway, subway, and industrial
infrastructure. Its location provides plausible cover for excavation, electrical use,
freight movement, ventilation equipment, and underground noise.

Although it is built after the war, parts of it may incorporate older wartime tunnels,
quarries, shelters, drains, or unfinished civil works. Later additions surround the
original core, producing the layered architecture encountered in the game.

Its original security doctrine assumes a genuine end-of-the-world event. Approaches
contain prepared demolition chambers so they can be collapsed outside the main blast
doors. The purpose is to stop blast pressure, contamination, invading forces, or
desperate surface survivors from following the preserved population inside.

### Population program

The founders believe that preserving existing society would only reproduce its
failures. Their intended successors must therefore be shaped from birth.

House Nine obtains babies through a mixture of apparently legitimate charity work and
serious crime: orphanages controlled by front organizations, falsified adoptions,
fraudulent death records, payments to desperate families, international relief
programs, trafficking, and kidnapping.

Each new society begins with infants and a small permanent founding generation of
adult caregivers. These adults are not outer personnel who commute between societies.
They enter their assigned society once, become part of its population, and do not
return to the personnel layer. They may be true believers, indoctrinated volunteers,
or people deceived about what they joined. Their prescribed history presents them as
the survivors responsible for raising the first post-catastrophe generation.

Later generations are born and raised inside. Eventually no living resident needs to
remember the true surface at all.

### Ideological fragmentation

The founders agree that they should control humanity's future but disagree about what
that future should be. Individual patrons and family factions gain influence over
different enclosed populations.

Each society becomes an attempt to answer one question:

> What weakness caused the old world to fail, and what system will prevent that
> weakness from returning?

Over time, the project changes from a continuity plan into a collection of increasingly
extreme social experiments. The founders die, but their heirs and institutional
successors inherit both House Nine and the conviction that its inhabitants belong to
them.

## Airtight personnel and resident separation

The separation between outer personnel and residents is a hard worldbuilding rule.
It prevents a universal staff culture from leaking into every society and allows each
population to develop its own understanding of the world.

### Physical arrangement

Resident societies are sealed habitats nested inside a surrounding service and
observation structure. Their walls, ceilings, utility spaces, and transfer rooms have
an exterior service face that residents cannot access.

There is no ordinary staff doorway into an occupied society. Under normal operation,
maintenance workers cannot decide to walk inside, and residents cannot unexpectedly
meet a delivery worker in a corridor.

### Supplies

Food, medicine, clothing, replacement tools, and other goods move through interlocked
transfer systems:

- A staff-facing service elevator terminates in a sealed provisioning chamber rather
  than opening directly into a resident space.
- Personnel load the chamber and close the exterior lock.
- Mechanical interlocks prevent the resident-facing door from opening until the
  personnel side is sealed and empty.
- Residents later collect the delivery from their side.
- Waste and damaged equipment travel through an equivalent reverse process.

Different societies may explain provisioning differently. It may be presented as an
automatic ark system, an inheritance left by the founders, a scheduled civic service,
or even a ritual whose real mechanism has been forgotten.

### Maintenance

Personnel maintain pumps, wiring, pipes, ventilation, sensors, transfer machinery, and
other infrastructure from the outer service shell. Equipment intended to be replaced
regularly is installed in through-wall cartridges or duplicated modules accessible
from the personnel side.

When something on the resident side must be handled directly, trained residents repair
it using parts and instructions passed through the transfer system. House Nine accepts
inferior repairs or loss of facilities rather than casually breaking separation.

### Observation and communication

Hidden cameras, microphones, sensors, sample lines, and observation apertures allow
management to monitor societies without revealing observers.

Instructions can be delivered through prerecorded announcements, altered voices,
printed material, indicator panels, or systems the residents believe are automatic.
No recognizable technician or administrator should become a common character shared
by all societies.

### Emergency response

House Nine responds to serious disorder by sealing compartments, altering power or
supplies, using resident authority structures, or deploying remote suppression
systems. Sending ordinary staff through a door is not the default response.

Any direct breach between the service shell and a society is therefore exceptional and
story-significant. The game begins during exactly such a systemic breakdown.

## Facility structure

House Nine follows an onion-like arrangement. The exact map is still open, but its
narrative layers are:

1. **External approaches** — Subway service tunnels, concealed freight routes, drains,
   and other disguised connections to the surface.
2. **Personnel and service annexes** — Garages, staff dormitories, kitchens, workshops,
   offices, storage rooms, briefing spaces, and maintenance infrastructure.
3. **Service and observation shell** — Hidden corridors and machinery surrounding the
   resident habitats without opening into them.
4. **Experimental societies** — Independent populations with separate histories,
   rules, leadership structures, and physical environments.
5. **Administration and control** — Records, surveillance, management systems, senior
   staff, and potentially an enclave for the current House Nine leadership.
6. **Original core and concealed infrastructure** — The oldest parts of the facility,
   which may contain the surviving escape route.

The organization may have constructed other Houses or prototypes elsewhere, but the
game does not require eight other playable sites. The precise origin of the number
`Nine` remains open.

## The outer staff annex: the existing hub level

The existing level is the player's introduction to House Nine and to the game's major
mechanics. It is large enough to serve a staff population of roughly twenty to thirty
people, not the facility's full resident population.

Its current spaces can be understood as follows:

- The tunnel office and workshop are an informal maintenance outpost used by Elin's
  uncle and other service workers.
- The blast door protects the staff compound from the external approach tunnel.
- The vehicle garage receives outside freight and staff vehicles.
- Containers hold supplies before they are inspected, repackaged, and transferred
  farther inward.
- The dormitory houses staff during extended shifts and sealed operating periods.
- The bathroom and industrial kitchen support the staff compound.
- The large dining hall and stage are used for meals, safety briefings, training,
  presentations, and staff functions.
- The more comfortable lounge is reserved for senior employees and trusted personnel.
- Air ducts and maintenance spaces provide alternate routes through the annex and
  teach the player how those traversal systems work.

### Why it is nearly empty

The story takes place over Christmas. Routine deliveries and nonessential maintenance
have been scheduled around the holiday, most outer personnel have gone home, and only
a small technical and security crew remains on duty. The resident societies are
already isolated and continue their normal lives without knowing why activity in the
surrounding service shell has temporarily decreased.

Elin's uncle chooses the Christmas staffing gap because it is the most predictable
opportunity he will get to act without coworkers noticing.

The first level can consequently contain no friendly character other than Elin. A few
hostile or incapacitated members of the skeletal crew may remain if an early combat
encounter improves pacing.

### Mechanical purpose

The annex should introduce mechanics through a relatively understandable environment
before the story reaches the stranger societies:

- Scripted movement and cutscenes.
- Injury, recovery, and health.
- Conversations and persistent dialogue topics.
- Object dragging and hidden items.
- Inventory pickup and object-on-environment use.
- Keys, cards, and keypad codes.
- Notes and optional environmental reading.
- Removable vent covers and duct traversal.
- Light manipulation and stealth.
- A limited early combat encounter.
- Exploration through interconnected staff rooms.

### Narrative purpose

The annex introduces the conspiracy through optional evidence rather than requiring a
long exposition scene. Possible evidence includes:

- Rules forbidding discussion of weather, dates, news, or surface life near transfer
  systems.
- Warnings against attempting to communicate through provisioning chambers.
- Supply manifests containing children's medicine, educational materials, and
  quantities far beyond staff requirements.
- Instructions to remove brands, dates, and commercial packaging from incoming goods.
- References to cultural contamination and population stability.
- Maps with occupied areas omitted or replaced by coded designations.
- An employee leave roster showing that Elin's uncle never signed out.
- Maintenance records for demolition circuits believed to be obsolete.
- Christmas leave notices, reduced delivery schedules, and a duty roster showing the
  small crew expected to remain in the annex.
- Old photographs of the founders and successive construction phases.
- Personal evidence that the office or workshop belonged to Elin's uncle.

Players who read everything should understand more of House Nine before entering the
first society. Players who ignore the documents should still learn the essential truth
through later events and conversations.

The copy of *1984* and the code `1984` are Elin's uncle's deliberate joke or warning
about House Nine's authoritarianism. The number does not need to be the year the
project began.

## Principal characters

### James

James is a municipal electrical, transit, or infrastructure technician. The exact job
title remains open, but it should explain why he is sent into a disused subway service
tunnel to investigate an abnormal power draw or failure.

He is practical rather than secretly important. His experience makes it believable
that he can work with breakers, tools, machinery, access systems, and improvised
repairs. He begins with no knowledge of House Nine and initially wants only to find a
route out.

His presence becomes destabilizing because he is living evidence that the surface is
inhabited. His longer arc concerns what responsibility he accepts for populations he
did not intend to discover.

### Elin

Elin is a reporter investigating hints supplied by her uncle. She enters the subway on
purpose but initially presents herself as an ordinary passenger caught in an unexpected
blackout.

A possible early version of her cover story is:

> I was waiting at the station when everything went dark. There were alarms and people
> pushing toward the exits. A man in a transit jacket opened a service gate and told us
> to keep moving. I became separated in the tunnels.

The later truth is closer to:

> There was no transit worker. My uncle told me when the power would fail and which
> service door would release. I thought I had a short window to meet him and get back
> out.

She withholds the truth because she entered illegally, does not know whether James is
connected to House Nine, and has been warned that the organization uses ordinary-looking
employees and observers. She may also possess notes, a copied key, press credentials,
or other evidence she initially keeps hidden.

Elin is not intended to follow James through every dangerous space. She can remain in
secured areas, study evidence, interview people, develop new conversation topics, and
help the player compare contradictory accounts. As the game progresses, each secured
location can become a temporary conversational hub.

### Elin's uncle

Elin casually describes her uncle as a janitor, but his work includes maintenance,
cleaning, transfer systems, and service infrastructure within the personnel shell. He
does not normally enter resident societies.

He initially understands only the compartment of House Nine needed to perform his job.
Over time, unusual supply requirements, censored work orders, hidden observation
equipment, personnel records, or an accidental systems breach reveal what the facility
contains. He realizes that his work has helped maintain a prison.

He decides to open an exit and expose the project. His attempt is morally courageous
but poorly controlled: it provokes the Last Wall response, traps Elin, nearly kills
James, and may destabilize multiple societies.

His fate remains unresolved. If alive, he should not be a spotless hero. He remained
silent and maintained House Nine for years before acting. Management can truthfully
call him a saboteur even while lying about almost everything else.

### House Nine management

The original founders are mostly or entirely dead. The current leadership consists of
their heirs, trusted institutional successors, and senior administrators who inherited
both the project and its assumptions.

Management regards the resident populations as humanity's future, scientific work,
family legacy, and private property. Different leaders may still favor different
societies. A later confrontation should include senior House Nine figures rather than
leaving the organization as a purely historical abstraction.

Their strongest defense is that abruptly opening the societies may cause violence,
psychological collapse, medical crises, and conflict between incompatible populations.
That concern can be partly true without justifying captivity.

## Opening incident

The current opening sequence remains usable with revised dialogue and context.

1. Christmas leave and reduced holiday operations leave only a skeletal crew in the
   outer personnel areas.
2. Elin's uncle remains behind and prepares a distant exit.
3. Elin waits at the subway location and time specified by her uncle.
4. James enters the service network to investigate an electrical irregularity.
5. The uncle diverts power and overrides the distant exit controls.
6. The subway station experiences an unexpected blackout.
7. Elin uses the brief power failure to enter the service tunnels.
8. Management detects the uncle's exit breach and activates Last Wall.
9. Prepared charges collapse multiple external approach tunnels.
10. James runs from the collapse, is injured, and loses consciousness.
11. Elin finds the office and later encounters James around the corner.
12. She helps him to the bed, treats his injuries, and gives him an incomplete account
    of how she arrived.
13. James searches for another way out while Elin appears to remain behind.

The hidden key, *1984*, screwdriver, access card, radio, storage-room details, and other
existing props can all form part of the uncle's workspace or trail.

The large blast door leads into the larger staff annex, not directly into a resident
society. This allows the current map to remain a self-contained tutorial and mystery
chapter before the first major social encounter.

## Progression through the game

### Phase 1: The service annex

James and Elin establish that the tunnel collapse was deliberate, that the annex was
recently occupied, and that a much larger concealed population is being supplied from
inside it. They search for the uncle and a surface route but find that the surviving
paths lead deeper.

The level remains mostly empty and uses optional notes, maintenance records, spaces,
and a small number of threats to build anticipation.

### Phase 2: The observation shell

James enters the infrastructure wrapped around the first resident society. Hidden
observation and provisioning systems reveal how complete the separation is. This area
can provide the first direct view of residents before they are aware of James.

Crossing the final boundary is a major narrative event. James and Elin are not ordinary
visitors. According to the resident worldview, people like them cannot exist.

### Phase 3: The societies

James and Elin pass through distinct visions of future humanity. Exact societies are
not yet canon, but candidates include:

- A carefully assigned family society with controlled partnership and reproduction.
- A communal society without meaningful private ownership or private space.
- A ranked society where food, comfort, education, and status depend on continual
  performance.
- A society that suppresses aggression and disagreement through drugs and conditioning.
- A society that restricts language, personal names, history, or emotional expression.

Each society should initially provide something its inhabitants sincerely value:
safety, solidarity, purpose, predictability, material security, or affection. The cost
of that benefit becomes apparent through exploration and conversation.

The societies may have been taught different catastrophes and may disagree about the
date, the nature of the surface, and the purpose of House Nine. Bringing them into
contact should create conflict even before the complete truth is known.

### Phase 4: The uncle and management

At some point James and Elin find the uncle or conclusive evidence of his fate. They
also reach surviving administrators or the inner management enclave.

The uncle's version, management's version, and the residents' histories should overlap
without agreeing completely. The player should be able to understand why releasing
everyone at once may be dangerous while still recognizing that continued imprisonment
is unacceptable.

### Phase 5: Escape

The known approaches have been demolished, so escape requires discovering a route not
destroyed by Last Wall. Candidates include:

- An original construction shaft omitted from modern plans.
- A flooded drainage or reservoir route.
- A concealed evacuation lift reserved for the founders.
- An underground freight spur.
- A ventilation or exhaust complex large enough to traverse.
- A route Elin's uncle partially opened before the lockdown.

The exact route and final choices remain open. Reaching the surface should be entangled
with decisions about the residents, the evidence, Elin's uncle, and whether House Nine
can or should remain sealed.

## Threats and combat

The current zombie-like enemies are not required to be undead. The preferred direction
is that at least some hostile people are victims of House Nine's attempts at social or
chemical control.

A program intended to suppress aggression, fear, grief, or disobedience may instead
produce neurological damage, rabies-like violence, withdrawal psychosis, or catastrophic
responses in a subset of subjects. This is compatible with the broader premise but is
not yet a fixed explanation.

The three enemies currently placed in the garage may be moved. If retained as an early
combat encounter, possible identities include members of the skeletal security crew
exposed to a lockdown agent or people displaced by the uncle's system breach. Their
exact origin should remain ambiguous during the first encounter.

Combat should interrupt and pressure investigation rather than replace it. Light,
sound, doors, vents, inventory items, and environmental systems should sometimes offer
alternatives to direct fighting.

## Themes and tone

The central thematic questions are:

- Who has the right to design another person's life?
- Can safety or happiness justify denying someone knowledge and choice?
- What happens when a community learns that its history was manufactured?
- Is a stable but coerced society preferable to a dangerous freedom?
- What responsibility does an outsider acquire merely by discovering imprisoned
  people?
- Can different populations reconcile when each was taught that the others could not
  exist?

House Nine should be frightening without being uniformly miserable. Friendly
conversations, humor, music, ordinary belongings, affection, and moments of comfort
are important. The existing playful exchanges between James and Elin are compatible
with the intended tone. Warmth gives the player something worth protecting and makes
the controlled societies feel inhabited rather than decorative.

## Writing and level-design constraints

- Do not imply that a genuine surface apocalypse has happened unless this document is
  deliberately revised.
- Do not depict routine face-to-face contact between outer personnel and residents.
- Do not use a recurring visible staff caste inside every society.
- Founding caregivers who live inside a society are residents, not commuting staff.
- Treat every breach across the personnel/resident boundary as exceptional.
- Give each society its own origin story, vocabulary, norms, and visual identity.
- Avoid making every resident a liar or every society immediately and obviously evil.
- Essential plot information must not depend entirely on optional notes.
- Optional notes should reward curiosity with earlier context, nuance, and alternate
  interpretations.
- Preserve the existing level assets and spaces where practical by giving them a role
  within the staff annex.
- Keep Elin's initial lie understandable rather than malicious.
- Keep James an ordinary, capable outsider rather than making him secretly central to
  House Nine.
- Let dialogue, inventory use, environmental interaction, stealth, and combat all
  contribute to progression.

## Open decisions

The following questions should be answered as content development requires them:

- What is the organization's official and internal name?
- Why is the facility called House Nine?
- What is the exact present year and location?
- When did the first population enter House Nine?
- How many generations currently live inside?
- What exact date during the Christmas period does the story begin?
- Did management already suspect Elin's uncle before his attempt?
- Is Elin's uncle alive, dead, imprisoned, injured, or altered?
- What exactly did the uncle intend to open, and whom did he expect to free?
- Which experimental societies will appear, and in what order?
- What caused the neurological or violent enemies?
- Who currently leads House Nine, and which founder's vision do they favor?
- How much does outside management still know about the facility?
- What route ultimately leads to the surface?
- What decisions can James make about opening, preserving, exposing, or destroying
  House Nine?
- What becomes of the residents after the truth is revealed?
